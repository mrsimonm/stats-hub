=== SLIDE 9 ===
*description, examination and
evaluation of dependencies
(relationships, associations) between
two (or more) statistical variables
*depending on the primary goal of the
analysis we may distinguish:
*descriptive analysis
*inferential analysis
*predictive analysis
*causal analysis
9
=== SLIDE 10 ===
*Descriptive analysis
*the aim is to describe relationships
between statistical variables in the
analyzed data set; no generalization!
*Inferential analysis (see Lecture11)
*the aim is to draw generalizing
conclusions (about population or data-
generating process) using confidence
intervals or statistical hypothesis tests
10
=== SLIDE 11 ===
*Predictive analysis (see Lecture4, L5, L6)
*the aim is to predict (forecast)
*in regression analysis: values of dependent variable
for“new” (not being in the analyzed data set)
values of explanatory variables
*in time series analysis: future values of a time series
*Causal analysis (see Lecture11)
*the aim is to draw causal conclusions; to judge an
impact (effect, influence) of one variable on
another
*in regression analysis: factual interpretation of
estimated (partial) regression coefficients;
answering a question“What happens with variable Y
if variable X increases about one unit?”
11
=== SLIDE 12 ===
*from factual perspective, we may
distinguish two types of relationship:
*unilateral: in the analysis, one variable represents
the cause and the other represents the
consequence (e.g. relationship between the height
and weight of a man)
*mutual: in the analysis the variables are “equal”;
it’s not clear which is the cause and which is the
consequence (e.g. relationship between the
weight of a man and the amount of his fat)

*to analyze the dependence (relationship) between
one qualitative and one quantitative variable (or
two quantitative ones when one of them may get
only few values)
*we consider one variable (usually qualitative) to be
a factor (e.g. level of education, type of car, …)
operating at different levels (treatments)
*we split the data set (values of quantitative variable X)
into k groups according to these levels (treatments)
*the second variable X has to be quantitative (so that
the calculation of arith. means etc. can be made)

*i.e. we split the total variation (ssT) of
values of variable X into two parts
T TR Ess ss ss 
*ssTR represents the part called between
groups variation, i.e. the part of the
variable X’s values variation which can be
explained by the (different levels of) factor
*ssE represents the part called within
groups variation, i.e. the part expressing
the rest of variable X’s values variation
35
=== SLIDE 36 ===
*high ssTR and low ssE: the dependence
between X and the factor is considerable
(is strong) in the analyzed data set
*low ssTR and high ssE: the dependence
between X and the factor is not
considerable (is weak) in the analyzed
data set
36
=== SLIDE 37 ===
*ssTR = ssT and ssE = 0: the strongest
dependence, no within-groups variation
(all values in each of the groups are
equal)
*ssTR = 0 and high ssE = ssT : no
dependence at all (i.e. independence),
no between-groups variation (all group
arithmetic means are equal)
