*1) according to the decisive time period:
stock vs. flow time series
*stock (point) time series:
*indicator’s value refers to a specific time point
(e.g. the number of employees
in a company on the first day in a month, the
level of supplies at the end of a year,…)
*indicator’s value is not influenced by the
observation period (the level of supplies at: the
end of every year vs. the end of each month)
*we can’t add up their values (it makes
no sense)

stock vs. flow time series
*flow (interval) time series:
*indicator’s value refers to a specific time

number of marriages, …)
*indicator’s value is influenced by the
observation period (monthly number of
marriages < quarterly n. of. m.)
*we can add up their values (number of
marriages: Jan+Feb+Mar=Q1)

*time series may contain one or more
of the four following components: trt,
ct, st and et
*a trend component is the main long-
term movement in a time series
*trend component value in time t: trt
*may increase, decrease, or may be
constant
*its form may be linear or non-linear
30
=== SLIDE 31 ===
*a cyclical component refers to repetitive
movements (“waves”) in the analyzed time
series with periods greater than one year
*cyclical component value in time t: ct
*increasing and decreasing phases alternate
*sometimes are considered as part of the
trend component
31
=== SLIDE 32 ===
*a seasonal component represents repetitive
movements (“waves”) around the trend with
periods of one year or less
*seasonal component value in time t: st
*regular and repeating (e.g. during each year,
or week)
*caused mainly by seasonal effects but
sometimes also by human activities
*cannot occur in long-term time series
*trt, ct and st together are called the
deterministic component
