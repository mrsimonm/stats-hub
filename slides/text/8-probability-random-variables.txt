=== SLIDE 2 ===
*A procedure for which each possible sample of a
given size is equally likely (i.e. has the same
probability) to be the one obtained (selected).
*example of a population: deck of N = 32 cards
*example of a (random) sample: n (randomly)
selected cards
*the best from the theoretical point of view
*the way of sampling when every combination of n
elements has an equal probability of being
selected

=== SLIDE 3 ===
3
*with replacement:
*element (member) of the population can be
selected more than once
*probabilities of random events in different trials
remain the same (independent events,
independent random trials)
*without replacement:
*element (member) of the population can be
selected at most once
*probabilities in different trials differ (dependent
events, dependent random trials)
=== SLIDE 4 ===
*systematic random sampling
*only one element is selected randomly; further
elements are drawn systematically using a
constant skip method
*e.g. only every 10th element is selected
*stratified sampling
*population is divided into subpopulations (strata)
*elements of each stratum should be homogeneous
relative to the characteristic of the population under
consideration (e.g. gender, age, social class, …)
*sampling is done from each stratum
4
=== SLIDE 5 ===
*cluster sampling:
*population is divided into groups (clusters)
*some clusters are selected by simple
random sampling
5
*all elements in the selected clusters are
then used in the survey
*less reliable than stratified sampling
*multistage sampling: combination of
several sampling methods; often used in
practice
