## Summary

Statistics seminar on time series: weighted chronological mean, measures of dynamics, base and chain indices, and linear and quadratic trends. First written test set for the start of the seminar in two weeks.

### Weighted Chronological Mean
- Unequal gaps between values (31 vs 28 days) in a stock time series require the weighted chronological mean formula.
- Mean number of employees from 1 January to 1 May was approx 265 employees.
- Use days (or hours, minutes) as distance units; months would be wrong because they hide different month lengths.
- Small mistakes in days per month on tests are tolerated if the principle is right; notes of month lengths are allowed.

### Measures of Dynamics (Exercise D1)
- First difference 1999: 17 minus 15 = +2 employees; growth coefficient 17/15 = 1.13, about 13% increase.
- Mean difference (38 minus 15)/9 = 2.56 employees; mean growth coefficient is 9th root of 38/15 = 1.109 (10.9%).
- Prediction for 2009: 38 times 1.109 squared, about 47 employees, assuming past average change continues.
- Stock vs flow could not be classified here: data too vague (point-in-time values vs yearly averages).

### Base and Chain Indices
- Interpretations must state the period, the indicator, and which periods are compared; never memorize phrasing.
- Use F4 in Excel to fix the reference to the base period.
- Missing values found by rewriting indices with y definitions: y9 = 14, y10 = 17, y12 = 12, y11 = 20.
- Harder cases: invert a base index, or multiply successive chain indices to get a base index.

### Trend Functions
- Linear trend is a regression line with time t as the explanatory variable; quadratic parameters only via Excel.
- Car starters linear trend: about 154 + 20.82t (later read as 164); approx 20,820 more starters each year.
- Prediction for 11th year: 393,000 starters, assuming the linear trend continues.
- Newborns quadratic trend: 16,356 minus 1,265t plus 58.7t squared; adjusted R squared 0.91 vs 0.75 for linear.
