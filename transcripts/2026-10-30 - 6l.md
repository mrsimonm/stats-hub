## Summary

Lecture 6 on descriptive time series analysis: moving averages (simple and centered) and two methods for quantifying the seasonal component, the simple approach and the regression approach.

### Course Logistics
- Lecture 6 topics are the first ones for the second midterm test.
- First midterm test is in 2 weeks, after immersion week, covering topics up to lecture 5.
- Exponential smoothing exists and is powerful for short-term prediction, but is not covered further in the course.

### Moving Averages
- Smoothing method to express the trend when a trend function cannot reasonably be used; values are lost at both ends.
    - Cannot be used for prediction in this course; sensitive to outliers like any arithmetic mean
- Window length M: subjective without seasonality (higher M, smoother); with seasonality M must equal length of seasonality s.
    - Typical s: quarterly 4, monthly 12, daily 7; not all such series are seasonal
- Odd M gives simple moving averages (plain arithmetic means); even M gives centered moving averages in two steps.
    - Centered: average M values, then average pairs of results to return to real time points
- Semester-mean example with s = 2 removed the seasonal component completely; formulas unnecessary if the principle is understood.

### Seasonal Component: Overview
- Seasonal differences (absolute, additive decomposition, sum to 0) or seasonal indices (relative, multiplicative, sum to s).
- Seasonal differences are in the original units; always write units in test answers, a point is often lost there.
- Simple approach gives differences or indices, trend and seasonality separately; regression approach gives only differences, simultaneously.

### Simple Approach to Seasonal Component
- Four steps: moving averages with M = s; subtract or divide by trend; average per season; transform to sum 0 or s.
- Semester example: seasonal differences -0.47 (summer) and +0.47 semesters (winter); winter index 1.147, i.e. 14.7% above trend.
- Interpretations must state the period, the series and season, and above or below trend; understand them, never memorize.
- Seasonal adjustment: subtract seasonal differences or divide by seasonal indices; moving averages with M = s also adjust.

### Regression Approach to Seasonal Component
- Course version assumes additive decomposition, linear trend and s = 4 (quarterly data), using dummy variables x1, x2, x3.
- Steps: get d0*, d1, a1, a2, a3 from Excel; compute mean a bar (a4 = 0); Sd_j = a_j minus a bar, Sd4 = -a bar.
    - Final test (no Excel) gives the parameters; second written test with Excel may require all steps
- Accommodation nights example (28 quarterly values): Q3 highest, Q1 lowest; d0 recalculated from d0* to enable predictions.
